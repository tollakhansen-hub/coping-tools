# Something has happened — evidence review (7 November 2026)

**Scope:** Internal research record only. No changes to public instructions, evidence wording, PDFs, review dates or deployment. This is a targeted narrative appraisal, not a systematic review.

## Hobfoll et al. (2007): five essential elements

**Reference:** Hobfoll SE et al. *Five essential elements of immediate and mid-term mass trauma intervention: empirical evidence.* Psychiatry. 2007;70(4):283–315. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2007.70.4.283 ; PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18181708/

**Design and scope:** International expert consensus informed by research from related fields; not a randomized trial of psychological first aid or the coping.tools steps. Focused on disasters and mass violence rather than all forms of bad news, bereavement or sudden change.

**Five principles:** (1) sense of safety; (2) calming; (3) self- and collective efficacy; (4) connectedness; (5) hope.

**Applicability to existing route:** Checking safety and the next practical need is consistent with safety and efficacy. Identifying one manageable action supports agency. Optional contact with a trusted person is consistent with connectedness. No pressure to recount events is compatible with a non-intrusive support approach. Ordinary routines may offer familiarity, but Hobfoll does not test the specific 'keep one ordinary thing' instruction. The route does not need to explicitly promise calming or hope.

**Limit:** The framework does not demonstrate PTSD prevention, symptom reduction or efficacy of the exact prompts.

## Psychological first aid integrative review (2024)

**Reference:** *The Effectiveness and Implementation of Psychological First Aid as a Therapeutic Intervention After Trauma: An Integrative Review.* 2024. Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11370167/

**Design:** Integrative synthesis of **20 studies** selected from **4,735 records**, combining quantitative and qualitative evidence, with study quality assessment. The review addresses PFA interventions delivered after traumatic exposure, which are not equivalent to a brief self-directed webpage.

**Interpretation:** Some results suggest potential improvements in anxiety and adaptive functioning, but studies vary in design, intervention content, delivery, populations and outcomes. Do not infer robust prevention of PTSD or depression, or that PFA is a validated stand-alone therapy. Verify individual outcome tables and risk-of-bias assessments in the full paper before making any more specific effect claims.

## Preserve contrary and safety evidence

- Retain the route's **2026 simulated-stress group PFA experiment** with no overall benefit at two weeks and possible adverse signal in highly distressed participants. A simulated group intervention is not the same as one-to-one practical support or this webpage.
- Retain the **2022 PFA outcomes systematic review** and its limitations; do not imply a settled effectiveness literature.
- Retain NICE **NG116** advice **against psychologically focused debriefing** for PTSD prevention or treatment.
- Retain the warning that media-exposure research is observational and that necessary safety updates should remain accessible.
- Preserve immediate-safety advice, optional social contact, no forced disclosure, no claim to prevent PTSD, and no implication that the user must recover or make sense of the event today.
