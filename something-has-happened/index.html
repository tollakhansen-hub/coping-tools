Something has happened

Bad news, a loss or a sudden change can leave you upset, numb, confused or unsure what to do next.

Making sense of everything can wait.

Deal with the part of it that belongs to this moment.

For the next minute

Say only what you know

Put what has happened into one plain sentence.

“The thing that has happened is…”

Try to stick to what you actually know.

Not why it happened.

Not everything it might mean.

Not what happens next.

Just the fact in front of you.

Then ask:

Is there anything I need to do in the next hour?

If yes, choose one thing.

If no, you are allowed to do nothing about it for a moment.

If you have a few minutes

Separate now from later

Make two short headings:

Now

Later

Under Now, put only what genuinely belongs to the next few hours.

That might be one phone call.

Getting somewhere.

Eating something.

Finding one piece of information.

Telling somebody what has happened.

Under Later, put everything that does not need solving yet.

A complete plan can wait.

You are giving today less to hold at once.

You do not have to talk about it yet

You might want someone with you.

You might want to tell somebody what happened.

You might prefer some quiet.

Any of those can be reasonable.

There is no need to describe everything before you are ready.

If it is coming through a screen

If the event is something you are following in the news or online, decide when you will look again.

Until then, close the feed.

You are not pretending it did not happen.

You are deciding not to receive the same event continuously.

For the rest of today

Keep one ordinary thing

Do one familiar thing you would normally do.

  • Make your usual drink.
  • Eat something familiar.
  • Take a shower.
  • Walk the dog.
  • Put on clean clothes.
  • Go through part of your normal evening.

It does not need to distract you or make you feel better.

It is simply one piece of the day that still has a known shape.

Leave the meaning for later

You may eventually need to make decisions, understand what happened or work out what changes now.

All of that does not need to happen today.

For now, it can be enough to know:

Something happened.

I am dealing with what needs dealing with now.

The rest can wait.

About this tool

This tool is designed for short-term coping during a difficult moment.

Where it draws from an established psychological approach, coping.tools checks the evidence behind that broader approach and does not claim that this shorter adaptation has itself been clinically validated unless that has actually been studied.
