# Evidence record — Self-administered single-session psychological interventions (young people)

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-06  
**Status:** Supporting background evidence; no practical change recommended  
**Scope:** Internal evidence record only. Not a public efficacy claim.

## Reference
Ball, J., Meiser-Stedman, R., Thompson, Z., & Chiu, K. (2026). *Self-administered single-session interventions for mental health in young people: A systematic review and meta-analysis.* Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 67(11), 1918–1929. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.70192

## Design and findings
Preregistered systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of 25 randomized controlled trial publications (5,628 participants aged 8–25). Nineteen studies contributed to quantitative synthesis.

- Anxiety: Hedges' g = −0.24 (95% CI −0.41 to −0.07).
- Depression: Hedges' g = −0.11 (95% CI −0.22 to −0.02).
- Substantial between-study heterogeneity; prediction intervals included no benefit.
- Approximately 24% of included publications were rated at high risk of bias.

## Relevance to coping.tools
The review provides indirect evidence that complete, structured, self-administered single-session psychological interventions can produce modest average improvements in symptoms among young people. It supports interest in accessible self-help delivery, not the effectiveness of any individual coping.tools action. The five coping.tools guides target practical support in the moment, are substantially shorter than many studied interventions, and have not been evaluated as the same intervention packages.

Potentially relevant trial directions for subsequent evidence review:
- Behavioural activation: *I feel low or shut down*.
- Intolerance of uncertainty: *My mind won't stop*.
- Psychological flexibility and perceived control: *There's too much at once* and *My mind won't stop*.

## Limitations and safety
The review covers predominantly young participants, with varied settings, interventions, comparators and follow-up. Outcomes cannot be generalized to all ages, immediate relief, or long-term effects. Clinical, crisis and suicide-prevention interventions must not be simplified into unsupervised exercises without separate safety assessment. No evidence from this review alone warrants replacing an existing guide or making a treatment-effectiveness claim.

## Evidence decision
**Add as indirect supporting background evidence only.** No public wording or exercise change. No new efficacy claim.

## Outstanding verification
The original 25 publications were screened for relevance, but an independent full-text audit of all trials, their adverse-event reports and effect estimates, and independent reconstruction of exact membership of the 19-study quantitative synthesis remain incomplete. Do not label that audit completed.
