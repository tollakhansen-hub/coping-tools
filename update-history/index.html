What’s changed

A record of changes to coping.tools and why we made them.

Significant changes are listed below, newest first. This history begins in October 2026 and includes selected recent updates.

27 October 2026

More ways to seek support

Updated the support message across the site to recognise trusted people, peer supporters and health professionals. Emergency services are still clearly identified for immediate danger or a medical emergency. The wording is kept brief so it does not distract from the coping tools.

Later steps are optional

Clarified across all five tools that the later sections are optional. A visitor can stop after the first small step rather than feeling they must complete every exercise.

News and online updates within the existing steps

Moved the screen-related guidance in Something has happened into the few-minutes section. This keeps the guidance available when relevant, while preserving the simpler progression from immediate needs to later in the day.

A shorter homepage

Removed the “How coping.tools works” section because it repeated the introduction and took up too much space on phones. Moved the explanation of the routes, timing and short-term purpose to About, replacing an existing paragraph. Kept a brief reminder on the homepage that the tools do not replace medical or psychological care.

Update history added

Added this page and an “Update history” link in the footer, so you can see what has changed and understand the reasons behind it.

26 October 2026

Gentler, more concrete wording

Refined wording across the five tools and the pages that help you choose and use them. Instructions place less pressure on reaching a particular feeling and make the next small step clearer.

A way back from printed guides

Added a short web address to each printed guide, so readers can find the online page and its evidence and guidance.

More consistent offline appearance

Updated offline storage to include the homepage’s current stylesheet, helping the installed site keep its appearance when there is no connection.

About review dates

An update may improve wording, layout or functionality. The “Last reviewed” date on each tool records when its evidence and guidance were last checked.
