I feel low or shut down

Sometimes everything feels flat, heavy or far away.

Even small things can take a lot of effort.

Feeling better does not have to be the goal right now.

Start smaller than that.

For the next minute

Make one small contact with the day

If action feels like too much, you can stop here and rest. If it helps, lower the sound or light around you.

One ordinary option may be enough.

  • Open the curtain.
  • Take a drink of water.
  • Put your feet on the floor.
  • Step outside the room for a moment.
  • Wash your face.

If one feels manageable, choose the least demanding one.

You can stop after that. The later steps are optional.

If you have a few minutes

A small action can come before motivation

You do not need to wait for motivation before trying one small action that takes only a few minutes.

Choose something small that meets a need, matters to you, or feels a little pleasant.

  • Put one thing away.
  • Make something to drink.
  • Change your clothes.
  • Walk to the end of the street or around the building.
  • Listen to one song you like.
  • Send one simple message to someone you already know.

Motivation does not have to come first.

You do not have to feel different afterwards.

You do not need to check whether it worked

When you finish, you do not need to immediately ask:

Do I feel better now?

The action does not have to produce a feeling.

If you did one small action, that is enough for this moment.

If the day still feels difficult

Leave one thing easier for later

If it helps, make one small thing easier for yourself a few hours from now.

  • Put clean clothes somewhere easy to reach.
  • Leave a glass beside the tap.
  • Put something simple to eat where you will notice it.
  • Place your shoes by the door.
  • Write down one thing you want to remember tomorrow. Leave the note somewhere you’ll see it.

Or choose one small action and a time to try it: “After lunch, I’ll step outside for a moment.”

You can stop there.

You do not need to repair the whole day.

The point is only to make one later moment a little easier.

If this feeling keeps going or makes everyday life hard to manage, consider speaking with someone you trust or a health professional.

If something else feels closer

  • There’s too much at once
  • My mind won’t stop

Need support? If possible, reach out to someone you trust or a health professional. In immediate danger or a medical emergency? Contact local emergency services.

About this tool

Behavioural activation has substantial evidence for improving adult depression, including in self-guided programmes. This page borrows its emphasis on manageable, meaningful action: a small action can come before motivation. The everyday options here are not a behavioural-activation treatment plan. Benefits in research reflect structured programmes and vary between people and settings.

A 2026 review included 105 trials with 13,933 participants overall. The main comparison in adult outpatients, using 61 trials, found a moderate average benefit compared with control conditions. Self-guided behavioural activation also showed benefits across 15 trials, with a smaller average effect. The broader review found benefits up to twelve months after randomization.

A 2025 review of digital behavioural-activation interventions found improvements in depression at two, three and six months and in quality of life at three and six months. It included 17 trials overall, with 12 contributing to meta-analysis. Its twelve-month depression analysis, based on two studies, found no clear benefit; this does not establish that quality-of-life benefits disappear at twelve months.

A separate brief online action-planning study found very small reductions in depression symptoms. It supports modest expectations for brief components rather than promising a mood change after one action. The examples here offer a manageable starting point, without requiring you to feel different afterwards.

Evidence and guidance

  • Digital behavioural activation — systematic review (2025)
  • Brief online behavioural activation — action-planning component study (2026)
  • Behavioural activation systematic review and meta-analysis (PubMed)
  • NICE — Depression in adults: treatment and management
  • WHO — Psychological self-help interventions
