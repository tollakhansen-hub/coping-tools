I feel low or shut down

Sometimes everything feels flat, heavy or far away.

Even small things can feel like more effort than they should.

Feeling better does not have to be the goal right now.

Start smaller than that.

For the next minute

Make one small contact with the day

Choose one ordinary thing around you.

  • Open the curtain.
  • Take a drink of water.
  • Put your feet on the floor.
  • Step outside the room for a moment.
  • Wash your face.

Pick whichever feels easiest.

Do just that.

Let that be enough.

If you have a few minutes

Do something before you feel like doing it

Choose one small action that would take only a few minutes.

Not something impressive.

Something ordinary.

  • Put one thing away.
  • Make something to drink.
  • Change your clothes.
  • Walk to the end of the street or around the building.
  • Send one simple message to someone you already know.

Motivation can come later.

Start with the action and let your mood do whatever it does.

Don’t check whether it worked

When you finish, try not to immediately ask:

Do I feel better now?

The action does not have to produce a feeling.

You got up.

You made contact with something outside the low or shut-down state.

That is enough for this moment.

If the day still feels difficult

Leave one thing easier for later

Do one small favour for yourself a few hours from now.

  • Put clean clothes somewhere easy to reach.
  • Leave a glass beside the tap.
  • Put something simple to eat where you will notice it.
  • Place your shoes by the door.
  • Write down the one thing tomorrow actually needs from you.

Then stop.

You are not trying to repair the whole day.

You are making the next small part of it easier to enter.

If something else feels closer

  • There’s too much at once
  • My mind won’t stop

About this tool

Behavioural activation has a substantial treatment evidence base for depression; a 2026 meta-analysis included 105 randomized trials. This page borrows only the practical principle that action can come before motivation. It is not a shortened form of treatment, and these brief exercises have not been validated as treatment.
