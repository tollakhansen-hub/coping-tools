For healthcare professionals & organisations

Practical support, freely available.

The five coping guides on coping.tools offer small, practical steps for difficult moments — when someone feels overwhelmed, restless, caught in repetitive thoughts, low in energy or affected by a difficult event. People can use the guides themselves, with support if wanted.

Healthcare professionals are welcome to use the guides in the care and support they provide.

  • My body won't settle
  • My mind won't stop
  • There's too much at once
  • I feel low or shut down
  • Something has happened

Free to use · No sign-up · Printable guides

Using the five coping guides in care

You can explore an exercise together during an appointment or supportive conversation, help someone choose a manageable next step, or offer a guide for them to use later if they wish.

Offer a guide as a choice. Ask whether practical help, quiet or company would be more useful.

View the five coping guides →

Each of the five coping guides includes Print and Save PDF options near the top of its page. Printable versions focus on practical guidance without website navigation; evidence notes, sources and limitations remain available online.

The guides are optional aids to support care. They are not clinical treatment protocols and do not replace assessment, professional judgement or appropriate care.

For information about how the guides are selected and reviewed, see How these tools are chosen →.

Practical support in everyday care

This companion resource is for professionals, carers and others supporting someone through difficult moments. While the five coping guides offer steps for the person using them, the companion focuses on what a supporter can change, offer or do alongside someone: understanding needs, adjusting surroundings, offering help and exploring activities together.

Use it alongside a coping guide or on its own. The person's needs and preferences guide the support you offer.

The companion offers five printable sections and one combined PDF. Its Research and evidence section explains the guidance and sources behind it.

Sharing and embedding

Healthcare services, schools, libraries, community organisations and others can share coping.tools with the people they support.

You are welcome to link to coping.tools, individual coping guides or the companion resource from your website, resource directory or information materials. There is no charge for linking, and no permission is required.

Interested in making selected exercises available directly on your own website? We are open to discussing suitable embedding or integration options with organisations.
